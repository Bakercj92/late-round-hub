How to read this

  • The measure changed, and that is what moved the answer. This board scores seats on making the playoffs (7 of 14 qualify) and winning the bye (the 1-seed plays 2 games, seeds 2–7 play 3) — not on expected VOR, and not on championship odds, which were measured against an opponent field that turned out to be a pushover. Seat 1 = % berth, points clear of seat 14; bye % vs %.
  • Seats 7–14 are one seat. The whole band spans points of playoff odds with the ordering scrambled — seat 10 lands ahead of seat 8, and seat 9 lands behind seat 10, which is the signature of noise rather than structure. The old "avoid 13–14" verdict is retired. It rested on a P10 spread from a value curve that overstated the top of the board and an opponent field that never compared value across positions; neither survived re-testing.
  • What did survive: seats 1–3 are genuinely better, and seat 1 most of all. But price it honestly — 7.7 points of playoff probability is roughly one extra playoff appearance every thirteen seasons. Chase seat 1 if it's free; don't pay much for it.
  • The value curve was recalibrated. It now prices a player at E[points | his ADP] rather than handing the player drafted RB1 the RB1 finisher's points with certainty. VOR figures on this page are therefore smaller than the old board's and are not comparable to it. Shrink ρ=0.65, chosen against published ADP-to-finish hit rates. ρ=0.50 is carried as a sensitivity: every conclusion here survives it — 1–6 gradient, 7–14 plateau, seat 1 on top — but the size does not. At ρ=0.50 seat 1 is worth 4.3 points over seat 14, not 7.7, which is one extra playoff appearance every 24 seasons rather than every 13. Read 7.7 as the top of a 4–8 point range.
  • The opponent field can now draft. 5 of 13 managers use a lineup-aware, value-aware brain with their real position tendencies as a prior. Calibration gate: your team ranks of 14 at seat 7 and takes % berth — both inside the honest range. Under the old field you were the best team in the room 67% of the time, which is why no probability from it was publishable.
  • Season variance IS in these numbers (40 outcome worlds per draft, drawn from the same curve, shared across seats so the comparison is paired). Per-position weekly dispersion is not — that's the next build.
  • Numbers: berth and bye from N= drafts/seat × outcome worlds, paired 95% CI ±0.9–1.0pp; the rounds 1–3 landing chips are a separate -draft pass.
